Reading
-------
1. Fresh cell ran and wrote a 4,005-byte run_log.json.
2. The intact cell was SKIPPED — the orchestrator logged the skip and did not
   re-enter the search.
3. The truncated cell (2,002 bytes, cut mid-string inside "config_sha256") was
   DETECTED as corrupt, DELETED, and RE-RUN, restoring a valid 4,005-byte file.

Why truncation and not deletion is the right corruption to model: an OOM or
wallclock kill lands mid-`json.dump`. The resulting file still satisfies
`Path.exists()`, so an existence-only resume check would skip a cell that holds
half a record. The orchestrator validates content, which is what these three
observations confirm.
